# Combating Online Harassment, Cyberbullying, and Impersonation

## 1. What Constitutes Online Harassment and Stalking?
Online harassment involves persistent, unwanted, threatening, or derogatory communications intended to intimidate, humiliate, or control a victim. It includes:
- Sending unsolicited sexual, violent, or abusive direct messages or comments.
- Doxxing: Publicly posting personal phone numbers, home addresses, family details, or workplace information.
- Mass reporting / brigading: Coordinated campaigns targeting an individual's profiles.
- Cyberstalking: Monitoring online activity, location check-ins, or contacting friends/colleagues.

## 2. Immediate Tactical Response Steps
1. **Prioritize Personal Safety**: If the harasser has access to your physical location or has made specific threats of bodily harm, contact emergency police (112) or women helpline (181) immediately.
2. **Do Not Engage or Retaliate**: Avoid responding angrily, pleading, or negotiating. Harassers thrive on victim reactions. Further engagement can also be twisted to accuse the victim of mutual confrontation.
3. **Preserve Evidence Before Blocking**:
   - Take full-screen screenshots displaying sender handles, date, exact timestamp, and the abusive text.
   - Copy the direct profile URL and post URLs.
   - Archive original emails (with complete RFC headers) or audio/video messages.
4. **Enforce Platform Controls**:
   - Restrict or mute the offending account first (which prevents them from knowing you took action while silencing their notifications).
   - Block the user and report their profile under "Harassment or Bullying".
   - Turn on comment filtering and set account to private temporarily.

## 3. Fake Profiles and Impersonation
If an imposter creates an account pretending to be you using your photos and name:
1. Do not report it casually; use the dedicated **Impersonation Reporting Form** provided by the platform:
   - Instagram/Facebook Impersonation Form: Requires submitting government photo ID proving your legal name matches the person depicted.
   - X (Twitter) Impersonation Policy: Direct form to submit identity documentation.
2. Ask trusted friends to report the profile for "Pretending to be someone I know", but avoid mass public call-outs that might drive traffic to the fake account.
3. Lodge an official complaint on `https://cybercrime.gov.in` under "Report Cyber Crime Against Women/Children" -> "Impersonation / Fake Profile".

## 4. Relevant Legal Provisions in India:
- **Section 354D of Indian Penal Code (IPC)**: Cyberstalking; penalizes monitoring women's internet usage or contacting them despite clear disinterest. Punishable by up to 3 years imprisonment on first conviction.
- **Section 509 IPC**: Word, gesture, or act intended to insult the modesty of a woman.
- **Section 66C Information Technology Act**: Identity theft; fraudulent use of another person's password, digital signature, or unique identification feature.
- **Section 66D IT Act**: Cheating by personation by using computer resource.
